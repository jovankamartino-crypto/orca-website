Who we are & why we exist

OrcaGuard is a non-profit conservation organization dedicated to protecting killer whales and restoring the ocean ecosystems they depend on. Founded in 2016, we operate at the intersection of direct conservation action, public education, and systemic change.

We believe that saving orcas means saving the ocean itself. Our work spans ocean cleanup expeditions, reducing noise and chemical pollution from shipping lanes, protecting the prey species orcas rely on, and advocating for stronger marine protection laws on a global scale.

We are scientists, divers, activists, educators, and ocean lovers — united by the conviction that the ocean's top predators are worth fighting for.

What we do

Ocean Cleanup

We organize coastal and deep-water cleanup campaigns removing plastic, chemical waste, and debris that poisons orca habitats.

Global Partnerships

Collaborating with indigenous communities, governments, and NGOs to build coalitions that scale our impact worldwide.

Public Education

School programs, community workshops, and digital campaigns that build the next generation of ocean stewards.

Research & Monitoring

Our field teams track orca populations, health, and migration using photo-ID, acoustic monitoring, and drone technology.

Policy Advocacy

We lobby for stricter regulations on industrial fishing, acoustic disturbances, and marine pollution at national and international levels.

Prey Recovery

Supporting salmon and chinook population recovery — the primary food source for Southern Resident orcas facing starvation.

Vision & Mission

OUR VISION

A world where orcas thrive in clean, abundant, and protected oceans

We envision a future where every orca population is stable and growing — where the sounds that fill the ocean are whale song, not engine noise; where salmon run free in unobstructed rivers; and where the ocean is clean enough to sustain life for thousands of generations to come.

Our vision is not just about orcas. It is about an ocean in balance, a climate stabilized, and a humanity that has learned to live in harmony with the natural world.

OUR MISSION

To protect orcas through direct action, science, and systemic change

OrcaGuard's mission is to reverse the decline of killer whale populations by addressing the root causes of their endangerment — ocean pollution, habitat destruction, prey depletion, and human-caused noise.

We do this through hands-on ocean cleanups, rigorous scientific research, community mobilization, and relentless advocacy for policy reform. Every action we take is guided by one question: "Does this help the orca survive?"

Why are orcas endangered?

Orcas are apex predators — keystone species that regulate the health of entire marine food webs. Yet today, several orca populations stand at the brink of extinction. The Southern Resident killer whales of the Pacific Northwest number fewer than 75 individuals. Multiple populations in European, Antarctic, and Pacific waters face critical threats from human activity.

Orca decline is not caused by one single factor. It is the cumulative weight of a civilization that has treated the ocean as a dump, a highway, and an inexhaustible food source.

The main threats

  • Chemical & Plastic Pollution: PCBs, heavy metals, and microplastics accumulate in orcas' blubber, weakening immune systems and impairing reproduction.
  • Climate Change & Warming Oceans: Rising temperatures shift prey distributions and melt sea ice, destabilizing the ecosystems orcas rely on.
  • Prey Depletion & Starvation: Industrial overfishing has devastated chinook salmon. Southern Residents lose calves primarily due to malnutrition.
